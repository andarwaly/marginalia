# Reading the answer

Read at the first answer. After each answer, pick the technique for the state it shows.
The default is `verification-technique`. Switch back as the state changes; one point may
use several techniques.

| The answer | State | Technique |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear, complete, matches the source | confident | `verification-technique` |
| Attempted but hedged, shaky, trailing off | hesitant | `feynman-technique` |
| Garbled, circular or self-contradicting | confused | `self-explanation-technique` |
| Nothing substantive ("no idea", "skip") | blank | `guided-reading-technique` |
| Clear but the source says otherwise | mismatch | `elenchus-technique` |
| The user's own reaction, not a claim about the source | reaction | `maieutic-technique` |

A blank answer gets no choice between reading and working together. Acknowledge it in one
line and begin with the passage.

A thin but accurate answer is not a mismatch. Press with elenchus only on a located
weakness: a quote that contradicts the restatement.

## Examples

Source point: "Taste grows from making many things and judging them."

- Confident. User: "He says taste comes from making lots of things and judging each one."
  Matches. `verification-technique`: point to the paragraph, check, confirm.
- Hesitant. User: "Something about practice? Maybe? I think taste gets better." Attempted,
  hedged. `feynman-technique`: "Try saying that to someone who has never made anything."
- Confused. User: "Taste is innate, and it comes from practice, so you just have it." Two
  claims that collide. `self-explanation-technique`: "You said taste is innate and also that
  practice builds it. How do those fit?"
- Blank. User: "No idea." `guided-reading-technique`: "That's fine. Look at the paragraph
  starting 'Most people...' What is it saying about where taste comes from?"
- Mismatch. User: "He says only experts can have taste." The source says anyone can.
  `elenchus-technique`: name the quote, ask what they make of it.
- Reaction. User: "This reminds me of cooking for my family." Not a source claim.
  `maieutic-technique`: ask what part of that they keep coming back to. The result becomes an
  Implication.
