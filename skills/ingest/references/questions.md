# Questions

Read at step 4. Every question to the user is one frame below. The user may read English
as a second language: a question says in plain words what it asks and what kind of answer
it wants.

## Rules

- **One object.** A question names its object in the same sentence: a line shown, a
  paragraph by its opening words, a demo by its caption, a row by its text. A claim passage
  is shown whole, because its opening words are its claim.
- **Asked once.** Before each question, search everything the user has said. When they
  have already given this answer about this object, in any words, go to the next move and
  count the question as answered. A passage shown in the last turn is named by its opening
  words, not shown again.
- **Order.** Frames 0 and 9 need no text from the passage: the user answers before meeting
  the author's words. Every other frame follows the author's line, shown first. Show a
  claim, then ask frame 1 or 2.
- **Length.** Fifteen words or fewer, plus the shown object.
- **Pointing back.** "That", "this" and "the last line" sit beside the object they name,
  in the same sentence.
- **Bridge.** Quote a short phrase of the user's only when it is the reason for the next
  passage, and state that link in one clause: `You said "articulate the reason". This demo
  shows how he does it:`. At most one reply in four opens this way; the others open on the
  passage or the question.
- **Repair.** A request to clarify ("what do you mean", "I didn't get it") means the
  question was unclear. Show the object, ask the plainer question, and say nothing in
  defence of the first. After the first request in the session, show the author's line
  before every question.

## Frames

| # | Frame | The answer | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | "When an animation feels right to you, where does that feeling come from?" | The user's own view | A thread's opening question, or any question that needs no passage text. |
| 1 | "In your own words, what does this line say?" | A paraphrase | A claim passage, shown whole first. |
| 2 | "Do you agree? Tell me why." | A stance and its reason | After a paraphrase, or on a shown claim. |
| 3 | "What do you see in the Linear logo when you move the mouse?" | An observation | A demo or image: name what to look at. |
| 4 | "What is the line 'Default → ease-out' for?" | A function | One rule or row, shown. |
| 5 | "Why do you think he numbers the typography rules?" | A reason | A format choice. |
| 6 | "How is the row 'Element appears from nowhere' like the scale animation?" | A connection | Two passages. |
| 7 | "He says 'any taste decision'. You said 'usually'. What is different?" | A difference | The user's words against the author's. |
| 8 | "You want to 'outsource creativity'. What does the easing flowchart teach you about that?" | An application | The Intention link, once per session. |
| 9 | "What are the main things this source says, in a few lines?" | The user's Spine | Once, at step 4. |

## Phrases the frames replace

"What do you make of", "sit with", "where does … fit", "draws the line", "part ways",
"bear on", "resonate", "land", "unpack", "thread" and "take" as a noun, "that step", "the
person doing it". A reader whose first language is not English cannot read meaning from
the words of these.
